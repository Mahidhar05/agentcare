WHEN TO GO TO EMERGENCY:

CALL AMBULANCE IMMEDIATELY IF:
- Chest pain or pressure
- Difficulty breathing
- Signs of stroke (FAST test)
  * Face drooping
  * Arm weakness
  * Speech difficulty
  * Time to call emergency
- Severe bleeding
- Loss of consciousness
- Severe head injury
- Suspected fracture
- Severe burns
- Poisoning or drug overdose
- Suicidal thoughts

COME TO EMERGENCY IF:
- High fever with severe symptoms
- Severe abdominal pain
- Persistent vomiting or diarrhea
- Suspected broken bones
- Deep cuts requiring stitches
- Severe allergic reactions
- Sudden severe headache
- Vision changes
- Seizures
- Pregnancy complications

EMERGENCY LOCATION:

WHAT TO EXPECT AT EMERGENCY:

Registration (Priority Cases):
- Verbal registration for life-threatening cases
- Full paperwork completed later
- No advance payment required for immediate care

Triage System:
- Red: Life-threatening (immediate)
- Orange: Urgent (within 10 minutes)
- Yellow: Semi-urgent (within 60 minutes)
- Green: Non-urgent (within 120 minutes)
- Blue: Chronic problem (routine appointment)

WAITING TIME:
- Life-threatening: 0 minutes
- Urgent: 5-15 minutes
- Semi-urgent: 30-60 minutes
- Non-urgent: 1-2 hours
- Depends on current case load

BRING TO EMERGENCY:
- Government-issued photo ID
- Insurance card (if available)
- Current medications list
- Emergency contact information
- Doctor's referral (if any)

- Insurance coverage available

WHILE WAITING FOR AMBULANCE:
- Stay calm
- Ensure patient's airway is clear
- Do not give food or water
- Do not move patient with suspected spine injury
- Note time of symptoms onset
- Gather medications and medical records

STROKE — F.A.S.T. TEST:
F - Face: Ask person to smile. Does one side droop?
A - Arms: Ask them to raise both arms. Does one drift down?
S - Speech: Ask them to repeat a phrase. Slurred?
T - Time: If any of these signs, call emergency immediately

HEART ATTACK WARNING SIGNS:
- Chest pain/pressure (may radiate to arm, neck, jaw)
- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweat
- Nausea
- Lightheadedness

REMEMBER:
- Time is critical in emergencies
- Don't wait for symptoms to worsen
- Call ambulance rather than driving yourself
- Insurance disputes handled after treatment
- Emergency care never delayed for payment concerns
